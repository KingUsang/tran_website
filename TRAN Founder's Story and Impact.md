# Founder Story & Impact Content Brief 

## **Founder’s Story** 

### **Suggested section heading: Why TRAN Exists** 

The Root Access Network (TRAN) was founded by Quadri Omoloju, a cybersecurity and technology professional who believed digital-safety education needed to become simpler, more engaging and easier to remember. 

After years of working in technology and cybersecurity, Quadri saw a recurring problem: the people who often need digital-safety knowledge the most — particularly children, families and everyday technology users — are frequently introduced to it through technical language, fear and information that is difficult to retain. 

**TRAN started with a simple question:** What if we could make cybersecurity knowledge accessible to everyone? 

That idea took TRAN into schools, streets and communities in Nigeria, delivering practical digital-safety awareness and learning directly from the people it wanted to serve. 

**Those experiences led to an even bigger question:** What if we could teach digital safety before bad online habits are formed — and make children actually enjoy learning it? 

That thinking became CYTRAC™, TRAN’s flagship educational intellectual property. Beginning with a cybersecurity board game, CYTRAC turns important digital-safety lessons into experiences children and families can play, discuss and remember together. 

Today, TRAN is building across the United Kingdom and Nigeria, combining cybersecurity, education, play and technology to create educational intellectual property with global potential. 

The vision extends beyond one board game. TRAN is building towards an ecosystem of games, stories, animation, school resources, digital learning experiences, competitions and other educational products designed to help prepare the next generation for life online. 

Alongside this commercial mission is the Ubuntu Bridge Initiative (UBI), TRAN’s social-impact arm, created to expand access to practical learning, mentorship, industry exposure and opportunities for emerging talent. 

##### **Our ambition is simple: build the world’s leading children’s digital-safety brand.**

## **TRAN Impact** 

### **Suggested section heading: From Communities to Classrooms to Products** 

TRAN’s journey has always been rooted in action. From taking digital-safety education into schools and communities in Nigeria to developing and testing educational products in the United Kingdom, TRAN is learning across two markets while building solutions designed to travel far beyond them. 

### **Nigeria — Community & Education Impact** 

#### **500+ students reached** 

More than 500 students have been reached through TRAN’s school-based digital-safety education and Cybersecurity Educational School Tour activities. 

#### **Digital safety taken into communities** 

Through initiatives such as Street Cyber Evangelism, TRAN took cybersecurity awareness beyond traditional classrooms and directly into communities, making practical online-safety knowledge more accessible. 

#### **Schools and educational engagement** 

TRAN has used interactive activities, games, demonstrations and awareness campaigns to help young people understand practical digital-safety concepts. 

### **United Kingdom — Product Development & Scale** 

#### **Building CYTRAC™** 

The United Kingdom is an important base for the development and commercial growth of CYTRAC™, TRAN’s flagship educational IP. 

#### **Real-world product testing** 

CYTRAC has progressed beyond concept and design into a physical educational product, including public playtesting in London. These sessions allow TRAN to observe how people interact with the game, gather feedback and refine the learning experience. 

#### **Building for global scale** 

From the UK, TRAN is developing CYTRAC beyond the board game into a wider children’s digital-safety ecosystem for families, schools and organisations internationally.

## **Social Impact Through Ubuntu Bridge Initiative** 

##### **Ubuntu Bridge Initiative (UBI) is the social-impact arm of The Root Access Network.** 

UBI exists to bridge the gap between talent and opportunity by providing emerging professionals with access to practical learning, mentorship, industry exposure and resources that can help them move forward. 

### **UBI Cybersecurity Internship — Cohort Impact** 

The response to UBI’s cybersecurity internship demonstrated the scale and international demand for accessible, practical career development. In less than three days after the application portal opened, UBI received almost 3,000 applications from over 50 countries. 

From those applications, 500 participants were admitted into three practical learning pathways: 

- Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) 

- Penetration Testing 

- SOC Analyst 

The programme is designed to move participants beyond theory through structured learning, practical exposure, mentorship and interaction with cybersecurity professionals. 

### **Removing barriers to participation** 

UBI’s impact goes beyond training. Where access to equipment or certification can become a barrier to progress, the initiative has begun providing targeted support.

## **Content Governance Notes** 

- Do not describe TRAN as primarily a cybersecurity training company. 

- CYTRAC™ must remain the flagship commercial IP and receive the strongest product prominence. 

- UBI must always be identified as TRAN’s social-impact arm. 

- Do not mix UBI internship/application metrics with CYTRAC commercial/product metrics without clear labels. 

- Use 'almost 3,000 applications from over 50 countries in less than three days' exactly unless a final verified application count or country count is approved. 

- Use '500 admitted' rather than implying all 500 completed the internship until completion data is available. 

- The 2 laptops and 5 certification vouchers are concrete support milestones and can be displayed as UBI impact. 

- Future CYTRAC products must be labelled 'in development', 'coming next' or 'our vision' until they are actually available.
