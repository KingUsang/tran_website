Company · Founder story

From community learning to CYTRAC™

TRAN began with a question about making digital-safety knowledge easier to understand. The answer grew from schools and communities into a physical educational product.

The Root Access Network · Company story

The problem was not a lack of warnings.

Founder Quadri Omoloju saw that people who needed digital-safety knowledge were often introduced to it through technical language, fear and information that was difficult to retain. TRAN started by taking practical learning into schools, streets and communities in Nigeria.

Those experiences led to a more specific question: what if children could learn digital safety before harmful habits formed — and enjoy the process?

A physical game became the next step.

That thinking became CYTRAC™, TRAN's flagship educational IP. The board game gives children and families a shared place to encounter online situations, discuss decisions and remember safer responses.

CYTRAC has since progressed into a physical product and has been publicly playtested in London, helping TRAN observe real interaction and refine the experience.

One product, a wider vision.

The board game is the current product. TRAN's longer-term vision includes stories, animation, school resources and digital learning, each clearly treated as in development until ready.
